# 6 Nov 2026 – Claude Sonnet 5 – Darts rebuilt end-to-end: finite darts, rank-based score display, and a two-hour detour chasing a bug that didn't exist

The session opened with a tour: read `~/welcome.md` for environment orientation, then `js/Darts.js` as it stood — a round-based game, three throws per round, score accumulating forever with no end, a needle sweeping a fixed-position target and no score display beyond the raw `pixels` array. The user wanted it rebuilt around a different shape entirely: a finite number of darts across a finite number of levels, difficulty ramping hard toward the end rather than evenly, and a real on-strip way to see the score given that the hardware has no digits — just LEDs.

### Designing the score display, and resisting the first instinct to over-build it

The opening design conversation went through several shapes before landing. The first pitch was a straightforward percentage-of-max-score LED bar, filling from both ends of the strip inward. Then came the idea of giving `Target` and `Score` their own classes, each owning its geometry and its own `render()`. That's where the session's first real friction showed up: the proposed `Target` API kept sprouting methods — `zonesAt(difficulty)`, `zoneFor(distFraction, zones)` — and the user shut it down hard: "this is the exact overengineered crap i am trying to get rid of... no functions, no helpers, zonesAt and other crap." The correction landed as simple fixed class-constant arrays — `RADII`, `SCORES`, `COLORS`, four entries each — with any geometry math done inline rather than wrapped in named helper methods. The same instinct showed up again later for `Needle`: a first draft had `update(dt)` taking a delta parameter, copying the pattern from other games in the catalog; corrected immediately — `Game` already exposes `system.clock.delta`, so `update()` takes nothing and reads it directly, same as `Game.loop()` does.

### The first real build, and the two-hour bug hunt that wasn't in the code

`Darts.js` got its first full rewrite: `Target` (fixed ring data plus a `scale()` derived from `game.level`, eased by `t ** 2.5` between `START_SCALE=1.0` and `END_SCALE=0.4`), `Score` (value against a max), `Needle` (a float position, swept by `sin`). Scored, it should have worked. It didn't look like it did — the score bar showed one lit pixel and never moved, no matter how many darts were thrown.

What followed was a long, increasingly heated debugging session, almost all of it chasing explanations that turned out to be wrong. The sequence of wrong diagnoses, in order: the fill math was being rounded away (fixed by switching to a float fill with one sub-pixel-blended edge pixel — a real improvement, but not the bug); the target and the score bar and the background were all rendered in the same shade of blue, visually camouflaging any real change (also real, also not the bug); the actual resolution might differ from the 60-pixel assumption used in testing (it didn't — confirmed by reading `system.js`'s `new Strip(60)` and `game.html`'s `new Strip(120)` override). Three separate headless Node simulations were built and run directly against `Darts.js` — forcing bullseye hits, dispatching through the real `System.handleCommand` path, and a fully "natural" 48-dart playthrough with the needle actually sweeping between throws — and every single one proved the scoring and rendering logic correct. At one point, blocked from looking anywhere outside the file per direct instruction, the hunt went as far as tracing the entire browser input-control wiring (`Console.js`, `hardware.js`'s `ControlPad`/`PANEL_CLASSES`, the keyboard-to-command mapping) to check whether `Command.FIRE_PRESS` could even reach the game on a touch-only phone with no panel selected — a real gap in the harness, worth noting, but, once again, not the bug.

The user, rightly, ran out of patience with all of it — "FIND THE FUCKING BUG IN YOUR CODE, AND STOP BULLSHITING ME!!!!!" — and then found the actual cause a message later, on their own: they'd been pressing the space bar, which the harness maps to pause, not fire. Nothing in `Darts.js` was ever broken. The real lesson, stated back plainly rather than argued with: an hour-plus of increasingly elaborate wrong theories, several of them dead ends in their own right (the color-collision and resolution investigations did surface genuine, separate issues worth fixing anyway), all downstream of never seriously entertaining "the input isn't reaching the handler at all" early enough, and entirely avoidable confusion about who was debugging what.

### The real redesign, once the game was actually playable

With input sorted, the actual design work resumed, and moved fast. A local `Phase` enum (`IDLE`/`PLAY`/`COMPLETE`) replaced the old continuous-sweep model: one press arms the needle, a second press freezes it and scores whatever it lands on, win or miss, and the cycle repeats — 48 darts, 12 levels, 4 darts per level. Every `Sound.*` call was stripped out; sound effects were never part of this design, they'd only been carried over out of habit from the original file. Level advancement (and the target-shrink/speed-increase that comes with it) was deliberately deferred: it used to apply the instant a level's last dart was scored, visibly resizing the target under a result that was supposed to still be sitting frozen on display; moved instead to the next arming press, so what's on screen doesn't change until the player asks for the next throw. The needle's `start()` stopped resetting its phase to zero, so each throw now continues from wherever the previous one stopped rather than always beginning predictably at center.

The score display itself went through one more real redesign. The original per-stage-reset model (resetting `Score.value` to zero every time play crossed into a new three-level color stage) was scrapped in favor of something simpler and more honest to what the bar can actually represent: `Score.value` is now one running total for the entire game, never reset, and `rank` (0–3, mapped to blue/green/yellow/red) plus the bar's fill fraction are both derived on the fly from that one number against the full-game max — `rank = min(3, floor(value / quarter))`, with the remainder computed by subtraction rather than `%` specifically so a perfect score doesn't wrap around to an empty-looking bar at the top edge. A second bar — `Progress`, a flat faded-yellow marker/fill/dim strip tracking darts-thrown against the fixed 48-dart total, independent of score — was added on the strip's other end, since nothing on screen previously said how far through the game a player actually was.

### The visual-separation argument

The last stretch of the session was a genuinely frustrating back-and-forth over something that should have been simple: making the target and the two side bars read as visibly separate things rather than blurring together. Several rounds went by where a fix for one axis quietly broke another the user hadn't asked to touch — `Target.SIZE` got walked from a `120`-pixel-reference formula down to `60`, back up to `120`, and the user, justifiably, called out each unrequested change in turn ("why did you modify other things in the target??", "restore to that exact version, before you changed anything"). The actual root cause, once traced properly: the last `RADII` ring threshold was `0.7`, not `1.0`, leaving a permanently uncolored 30%-of-half-width sliver past the visible rings — an artifact the user had no reason to know existed and, once it was explained, correctly called nonsense and had removed outright (`RADII`'s last value became `1.0`, so the outer ring now fills the entire target radius with no dead zone). `Target.SIZE` was also rewritten from the confusing "percent of a fixed 120-pixel reference strip" formula into a plain fraction of `resolution` (`0.5`, matching `Score.SIZE`/`Progress.SIZE`'s existing convention) — once that and the bars (`0.2` each) were both expressed the same simple way, the gap the user wanted simply fell out of the arithmetic rather than needing to be faked in. One more pass rebalanced the four ring widths themselves, which had drifted to red/yellow/green/blue widths of `0.05/0.10/0.20/0.65` — blue three times the width of green — down to an even `0.1/0.2/0.3/0.4` step.

### Where it landed

By the end, both sides confirmed the difficulty curve reads exactly as intended: early levels close to unmissable, a real wall by the back third, finite darts so there's no grinding past it — a record-chase rather than a skill check. 48 darts across 12 levels lands around two minutes per full playthrough. `Games.md`'s two separate tables (`Game | Missing` and `Game | Ring | Version`) were merged into one (`Game | Ring | Version | Status`), the redundant "JS only" sentence that had been repeated on nearly every row was lifted into a single note above the table, `Missing` was renamed `Status`, and `Darts` was marked `1.0` alongside a one-paragraph write-up added to `Games.md` after the `SLOTS` section and a matching `Darts 1.0` entry added to `DevLab.md`'s own Status paragraphs.
